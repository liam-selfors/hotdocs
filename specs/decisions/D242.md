# D242 — `spec-linter` is split into a corpus loader, two checkers and the linter

## Context

Regenerating `spec-linter` from its spec took sixteen attempts and kept
it at its 4,000-word cap, where every rule the hand-written linter had
applied silently needed an equal trim elsewhere to be written down.
`R-LINT-022` had flagged it as crowding four size dimensions at once,
the decomposition signal `SPEC_GUIDELINES.md` §4 describes, and `D77`
had deferred the split. One generation also had to reproduce thirty-two
rules exactly at once, so a single slip anywhere failed the whole
candidate.

## Decision

Three components come out of `spec-linter`:

- `corpus-loader`: `load_corpus` and the `Corpus` model (`D208`), which
  the linter, both checkers and the bundle assembler read.
- `citation-checker`: `R-LINT-026`–`030`, renumbered `R-CITE-001`–`005`.
- `size-checker`: `R-LINT-012`, `022`, `023` and `025`, renumbered
  `R-SIZE-001`–`004`.

Each checker exposes `check(repo_root)` returning plain finding dicts.
`spec-linter` keeps the remaining rules, the `LintReport`, the CLI and
`lint()`, which appends both checkers' findings (`R-LINT-033`), and
re-exports `load_corpus`, so `spec-bundle-assembler` and
`impact-analyzer` keep reading the corpus through it unchanged. A
finding's `rule_id` stays the requirement that produced it, so the moved
rules report under their new ids; references to the old ids outside the
decision records are updated.

## Consequences

`spec-linter` drops to about 3,000 words and 24 requirements. Its
remaining crowding warning is now about its generated code size, which
regeneration should reduce. The hand-maintained tests that name a moved
rule's id are updated when `spec-linter` is regenerated.
