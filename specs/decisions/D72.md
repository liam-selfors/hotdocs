# D72 — Decision atomicity and citation discipline

## Context

A trace of decisions bundling multiple unrelated topics risks becoming internally confusing over time, and a citation that is never checked against what it actually names risks quietly pointing at the wrong thing — both concerns found to be real in this corpus's own history.

## Decision

A decision record must cover exactly one topic, tested with a concrete question: does removing any one paragraph leave the rest meaning something different? A citation must point to the specific decision responsible for the specific fact stated, checked against that decision's own text before relying on it — never assumed correct merely because it resolves to a real file. The practical, zero-tooling drift check: `grep -rn '\bD<N>\b' specs/ --include="*.md" | grep -v '^specs/decisions/'`, excluding decision records themselves since a citation inside another decision's own frozen narrative is never a drift candidate.
