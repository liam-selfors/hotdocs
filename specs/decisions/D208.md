# D208 — `spec-linter`'s `load_corpus` and `Corpus` objects are part of its interface

## Context

`D101` made `spec-bundle-assembler` read corpus structure only through
`spec-linter`'s `load_corpus` and its `Corpus`/`Component`/`Doc`
objects, and `impact-analyzer` reads them too. `spec-linter`'s interface
named only `lint()`. While it was hand-written that never mattered; a
generated `spec-linter` could legitimately drop `load_corpus` or reshape
its objects and break both consumers, and because a bundle's
`spec_bundle_hash` covers the text read through those objects, a
different `body` rule would change every component's hash.

The attributes consumers read were traced at runtime, running the
assembler over every component and checked against both consumers'
source.

## Decision

`load_corpus(repo_root) -> Corpus` joins `interface/library.md`, and a new
`interface/data/Corpus.md` defines the three dataclasses' fields and
order, how each path maps to a `doc_type`, the frontmatter value rules,
the `body` and `doc_id` rules, every dict's keys, and `graph`.

`spec-linter` was at 3994 words of its 4000 cap, and `D77` chose not to
split it. Room came from rationale, not rules: `component.md`'s Scope
list (a restatement of `requirements/`), `AC-LINT-015`'s explanatory
paragraph, "confirming ..." tails on three criteria, and closing
explanations in `R-LINT-016`, `R-LINT-020` and `R-LINT-028`.
`interface/cli.md` now states `main(argv)`'s contract (the tokens after
`lint`, as `hotdocs-cli` passes them) in fewer words than before.
`component.md` also said every criterion was `kind: compiled`; 33 are
`generated-test` and two `llm-judge`, and it now says so.

## Consequences

The component stays one component at 3992 words. `R-LINT-022`'s
crowding warning still fires; the next addition needs the split `D77`
deferred.
