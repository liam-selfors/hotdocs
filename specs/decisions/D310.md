# D310 — A bounded attempt loop with requirement-id feedback replaces one-shot generation

## Context

`D3` removed every automatic retry: a candidate that failed verification
blocked the merge, and only a developer could try again, passing the
failed requirement ids by hand with `--retry-feedback`. Its reasons were
that a retry loop hid failures, could select for a lucky candidate, and
could leak acceptance criteria into the generator through the feedback.
Outside a supervised session nobody is there to retry, and the tool's
own regeneration needed one to three seeds for most components. The
leak is already closed (`R-GEN-012`: feedback is bare requirement ids),
and the conditions that made a retry blind are fixed: a failing
candidate is no longer cached (`D305`), a truncated reply is a named
failure (`D304`), and a failure caused by a broken test is marked as such
(`D307`).

## Decision

A new component, `generation-loop`, runs up to `max_attempts` (default
3) attempts of generate, surface check and verify. The next attempt's
feedback is the sorted, de-duplicated covered requirement ids of the
failed criteria whose `failure_origin` is not `"test"`, joined with the
caller's. Retried: verification and surface failures,
`truncated_response`, `missing_module`, `empty_response`, and an
agentic run that did not finish. Not retried: network, auth, rate-limit
and timeout errors, a refusal, and a sealed or pinned target. A passing
single-mode candidate is committed to the cache. Every attempt is
recorded, so `attempt_history` in the manifest shows how many tries a
landing took and with what feedback.

The selection risk is bounded: tests stay independent of the code, the
mutation bar is unchanged, and the attempt count is visible.

## Consequences

`D3` is deprecated. `component-gate-runner` uses `generation-loop`, and
`--max-attempts=1` restores one-shot behavior.
